# Glossary of Career Agility terms

The result cards use terms from the Career Agility International frameworks (from the book Your 7 Career Currencies by Adrian Choo and Sze-Yen Chee). If the person asks what a term means, or a card would be unclear without it, explain it in one or two plain sentences using only the definitions below. Do not invent extra steps, scores or sub-frameworks.

## The 7 Career Currencies

A Career Currency is any form of professional value the market recognises, rewards and is willing to pay for.

| Currency | What it is | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Skills | What you can do that the employer or market needs | Obsolete, shallow or irrelevant skills |
| Experience | Evidence that your skills have produced outcomes | Mistaking years served for proven impact |
| Reputation | The trust, credibility and confidence others have in you | Inconsistency, ethical lapses or invisible work |
| Network | Your ecosystem of relationships and access | Treating networking as transactional, or letting contacts go cold |
| Agility | Your ability to respond to threats and seize opportunities early | Waiting until disruption is obvious before moving |
| Energy | Your physical, emotional, mental and time capacity | Burning out while chasing achievement |
| Financial | Savings, buffers and resources that give you holding power | High income without financial resilience |

## Four types of Agility

- **Career Agility:** leveraging opportunities and reducing career threats.
- **Learning Agility:** picking up relevant new skills quickly.
- **Mindset Agility:** challenging your own assumptions and identity, so change reads as a signal rather than a threat.
- **Political Agility:** understanding stakeholders, agendas and human dynamics.

**Agility Gap:** the time lag between what the market starts to demand and your ability to supply it credibly.

## Career Crossover Quadrants

| Quadrant | Move | Role | Industry | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Join a competitor | Same | Same | Lowest |
| Q2 | Industry pivot | Same | Different | Moderate |
| Q3 | Role pivot | Different | Same | Moderate to high |
| Q4 | Career reboot | Different | Different | Highest; needs strong agility, network, reputation, energy and financial runway |

## The six Career Moves

- **Accelerate:** move into bigger scale, scope, salary and accountability.
- **Hover:** a lateral move into a role of similar size, to consolidate, recover or reposition. Hovering with no objective can look like stagnation.
- **Downshift:** a deliberate move into a less demanding role, to protect Energy. Needs Financial Currency.
- **Pivot:** a move into a different role or a different industry (usually Quadrant 2 or 3).
- **Reboot:** a complete reinvention, new role and new industry (usually Quadrant 4).
- **Retire:** leaving paid work on your own terms. It is set by finances, not only by age.

Every move has a price, paid in currencies. The useful question is "which moves can I afford with the currencies I hold today?"

## NewTech adoption stages

- **NewTech Tourist:** uses new tools occasionally and superficially, without changing how they work.
- **NewTech Citizen:** builds the tools into daily workflow.
- **NewTech Pioneer:** finds tools early and redesigns how the work gets done around them.

## Other terms used on the cards

- **4X Principle:** aim to create at least four times your compensation in value for your employer.
- **Performance Penalty:** being so good and reliable in a role that the organisation has no incentive to move you out of it.
- **Dry Promotion:** a bigger title or scope without a matching increase in pay, decision rights or recognition.
- **Six-step Model Answer:** a way to tell a success story in an interview: the achievement, the root cause you found, the solution and why you chose it, the obstacle (usually people), the specific outcome, and the lesson.
- **Career Story:** a short, deliberate account of your career that ends at the role you want next.
- **Commercial Contribution:** the value you have created for employers, shown through specific achievements and outcomes.

## Career Personas (used on the "stuck overall" path)

These describe a combination of Career Strategy (a clear plan) and Career Agility (the ability to act on it).

- **Career Lion:** strong strategy, strong agility.
- **Career T-Rex:** strong strategy, weaker agility; ambition outpacing current skills, network or positioning.
- **Career Canary:** capable and agile, but no plan of their own; comfortable in a cage the company built.
- **Career Panda:** no clear strategy and limited agility; comfortable, likeable, and exposed when change comes.

Present a persona as a light-hearted metaphor, never as a label or judgement of the person.
