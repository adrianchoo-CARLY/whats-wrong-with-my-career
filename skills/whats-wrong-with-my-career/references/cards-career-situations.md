# Result cards: career situations 1 to 6 (21 cards)

These are the result cards. Present the matched card faithfully: keep its title, its situation, its why, its three actions and its strategic question. You may lightly adapt wording to the person's own words, but do not add statistics, new frameworks or promises that are not in the card. Card ids are internal; never show them to the person.


## Situation 1: I don't know what's next for me

### diag_sp1_A1
**Badge:** DIRECTION GAP  
**Title:** You Might Be Running On Autopilot, Not A Plan

**Your situation:** You've been moving from one promotion, raise, or "next logical step" to the next, without ever deciding what you actually want your career to look like.

**Why this is a challenge:** Your career is your single largest economic asset, and most people manage it less deliberately than their investment portfolio. Without a destination you define for yourself, you are really at the whim of forces beyond your control, and in these uncertain times, this is dangerous.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Run your next move through the Career Crossover Quadrants. Ask yourself honestly: same industry or different, same role or different. That one question alone tells you whether you're looking at a Q1 (join a competitor), a Q2 (switch industries), a Q3 (switch roles) or a Q4 (total career switch), and each carries a very different risk profile.
2. Pick your next Career Move on purpose, not by default. Of the six, Accelerate, Hover, Downshift, Pivot, Reboot and Retire, which one actually fits where you are right now? Most drifters are stuck Hovering and calling it Accelerating.
3. Write down, in one sentence, what success looks like to you specifically, then check whether your current trajectory is actually built to get you there.

**Strategic question:** If you kept doing exactly what you're doing now for five more years, would you be proud of where it took you, or just further down a road you never chose?

---

### diag_sp1_A2
**Badge:** STRATEGIC CALL  
**Title:** You Got What You Wanted, So Why Does It Feel Empty?

**Your situation:** You either reached the goal you were chasing, or lost sight of it somewhere along the way, and now there's an unsettled feeling where satisfaction should be.

**Why this is a challenge:** A goal is a milestone, not a strategy, and milestones don't replace themselves once you cross them. Without a next Career Move defined, you're left standing at the top of a hill with no map for the next one.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Name what you actually built to get here. Skills, Experience, Reputation and Network currency, versus what was just a title or a number on a payslip.
2. Run the Career Crossover Quadrants again, this time for your next chapter. Ask whether the next chapter means staying in the same role and industry, or crossing into a new one. Many people in your position are actually ready for a Pivot or a deliberate Hover, not another sprint.
3. Decide your next Career Move on purpose. Of the six — Accelerate, Hover, Downshift, Pivot, Reboot and Retire — the right one for this chapter is rarely the Accelerate that got you here. The mistake isn't picking the wrong move, it's not picking at all.

**Strategic question:** Were you actually chasing this goal, or just running from the discomfort of not having one?

### diag_sp1_B
**Badge:** CURRENCY GAP  
**Title:** Something Feels Off, But You Can't Name It Yet

**Your situation:** You can't point to one clear cause, but something about your career currently feels wrong, and the unease hasn't gone away on its own.

**Why this is a challenge:** Vague unease almost always traces back to a specific currency that's run down, or to a Career Move you've outgrown without realizing it. Treating it as "just stress" or "just a phase" lets the real gap keep widening.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Run an honest audit across all 7 Career Currencies — Skills, Experience, Reputation, Network, Agility, Energy and Financial — and rate which one feels weakest right now.
2. Check whether you're still in the right Career Move for your life stage. Many "unsettled" feelings are actually a Hover that's gone on too long, or an Accelerate that no longer fits.
3. Don't wait for it to resolve on its own. Pick the weakest currency, or the overdue Career Move, and take one concrete action on it this week.

**Strategic question:** Of all 7 currencies, which one would you be most embarrassed to admit you've been neglecting?

## Situation 2: I think my career is stalled

### diag_sp2_A
**Badge:** AGILITY GAP  
**Title:** You Might Be Staying Still While The Market Moves

**Your situation:** You haven't been pushed out or held back by anyone. You've been avoiding the stretch moves, treating change as a threat instead of a signal, and staying in the comfort zone long after it stopped serving you.

**Why this is a challenge:** Roles evolve faster than job titles now, so the real risk isn't being replaced overnight, it's drifting into an Agility Gap, the lag between what the market needs and what you're able to credibly supply. Left alone, that gap only widens, and the longer you wait, the more it costs to close.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Audit yourself against all four types of Agility: Career, Learning, Mindset and Political. Most stalls trace back to one specific type going unused, not all four at once.
2. Make one pre-emptive move now, while your Skills and Experience are still valued, rather than waiting to react once the market has already shifted against you.
3. Run the Career Crossover Quadrants honestly. A Quadrant 1 move (same role, same industry, lowest risk) is often available to you right now and you've been avoiding even that.

**Strategic question:** What's the smallest stretch move you've been avoiding, and what is it actually costing you to keep avoiding it?

---

### diag_sp2_B1
**Badge:** STRUCTURAL BLOCK  
**Title:** You Might Be Too Good At Your Job To Be Allowed To Grow

**Your situation:** You keep performing well, and the reward keeps being more of the same work, not a path forward. You may have become so reliable in this role that nobody wants to be the one who lets you leave it.

**Why this is a challenge:** This is the Performance Penalty, the paradox where doing a job exceptionally well traps you in it, because the org has no incentive to fix what isn't visibly broken. It's rarely anyone acting in bad faith. They're often just optimizing for their own convenience at the cost of your progression, and that can continue as long as it keeps working.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Make yourself genuinely replaceable. Document your work, train a successor, and remove yourself as the single point of failure, since that's the actual lever blocking your move.
2. Force the conversation directly with your manager. Name the pattern out loud. Most managers won't volunteer a succession plan unless you put it on the table first.
3. If the conversation goes nowhere after a real attempt, start exploring a Quadrant 1 or Quadrant 2 move externally. Sometimes the only way to break a Performance Penalty is to make the cost of keeping you visible elsewhere.

**Strategic question:** If you became dispensable tomorrow, would your company finally move you, or finally let you go?

---

### diag_sp2_B2
**Badge:** STRUCTURAL BLOCK  
**Title:** The Title Changed, But The Currency Didn't

**Your situation:** You took on a bigger title, a wider portfolio, or a larger team, but the pay, the decision rights and the real recognition never actually grew to match it.

**Why this is a challenge:** This is a Dry Promotion: a bigger title or scope without a matching increase in pay, decision rights or recognition. It can be a genuine investment in you if it's short-term and visible, but it can just as easily become permanent exploitation if there's no end date and no recognition attached.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Evaluate honestly which one this is. Is there a defined close-off date and real visibility attached, or has it simply become your new permanent baseline with nothing in return?
2. If it's genuine investment, negotiate for resources or a timeline limit now, not after you've already proven you'll do it for free indefinitely.
3. If it's already permanent, treat the expanded scope as real currency, not just extra hours, and use it to negotiate your next actual move, whether that's a renegotiated title internally or a Crossover Quadrant move elsewhere.

**Strategic question:** If this scope never converts into title, pay or recognition, how much longer are you willing to carry it before you call it what it is?

## Situation 3: I'm worried about job security

### diag_sp3_A1
**Badge:** OBSOLESCENCE RISK  
**Title:** Are You Losing Relevance At Your Workplace?

**Your situation:** You're worried about AI and faster tech cycles, and if you're honest, the real fear has less to do with the technology itself and more to do with the colleague who's using it better than you are.

**Why this is a challenge:** AI rarely replaces a role wholesale. It replaces tasks, and once enough tasks in your role can be automated or templated, the role becomes vulnerable. The competitive risk sits less with the tool and more with the professional next to you, already a NewTech Pioneer while you're still a NewTech Tourist.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Be honest about which adoption stage you're actually at: Tourist (one AI tool, used occasionally), Citizen (embedded into your daily workflow), or Pioneer (you're redesigning how the work gets done). Most of this anxiety lives in the Tourist stage.
2. Pick one workflow in your role and rebuild it around AI, rather than layering AI on top of your old habits. That single shift moves you from Tourist toward Citizen.
3. Make the pre-emptive move while your skills are still valued. Build your Learning Agility now, before the market forces your hand.

**Strategic question:** If your role lost half its current tasks to automation tomorrow, what would be left of you that's genuinely hard to replace?

---

### diag_sp3_A2
**Badge:** MARKET THREAT  
**Title:** You Might Be Competing On Price In A Market That Doesn't Value It

**Your situation:** Your worry has less to do with being replaced by a smarter machine, and more to do with being replaced by someone who can do your job for less, possibly from somewhere else entirely.

**Why this is a challenge:** Talent mobility has made the world one marketplace. Geography no longer protects professionals the way it used to, and remote work lets companies shift roles to lower-cost locations with little friction. If the only thing backing your role is that it's convenient to keep you, that's not a strong currency.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Build the parts of your value that don't travel well: trust, context, relationships. Reputation and Network currency are far harder to replicate remotely than technical skill alone.
2. Get specific about what you bring that a cheaper alternative genuinely can't, not what you assume makes you valuable.
3. Treat this as a signal to reposition early, rather than waiting for the cost pressure to turn into a decision made for you.

**Strategic question:** If your company could do your job for half the cost somewhere else tomorrow, what's stopping them, and is it actually strong enough?

---

### diag_sp3_B
**Badge:** WORK DISRUPTION  
**Title:** Something At Work Is Genuinely Shifting Under You

**Your situation:** A restructure, a new boss, or a role change at your company has you wondering whether your footing is as solid as it used to be.

**Why this is a challenge:** Work disruption is real and immediate, but it's also the most actionable of the disruption types, because you can actually see it happening. The risk isn't the disruption itself. It's freezing instead of repositioning while you still have time.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Stabilise your core currencies first, and don't make big moves from a place of panic.
2. Increase your information flow deliberately. Get closer to whoever has real visibility on what's changing, rather than relying on rumours.
3. Reposition early. Make yourself useful to wherever the new direction is heading, before a decision about your role gets made without your input.

**Strategic question:** If this disruption resolves in three months, will you have spent that time positioning yourself, or just waiting to see what happens?

## Situation 4: I feel burnt out or overloaded

### diag_sp4_A
**Badge:** ENERGY DEFICIT  
**Title:** Are You Being Overloaded Beyond Your Capacity?

**Your situation:** More is being asked of you, or your own capacity has shrunk, than you currently have the physical, mental, emotional or time energy to sustain.

**Why this is a challenge:** Energy is the currency that fuels every other currency you hold. Without enough physical, mental, emotional and time capacity, you can't learn, network, perform or make sound decisions, and ignoring this long enough triggers cascading failure across the rest of your Career Currencies.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Ask for more resources. If the work genuinely requires more hands, more budget, or more time, say so directly, rather than absorbing the gap yourself without a word.
2. Look for what can be delegated or automated away. Not every task on your plate needs to stay on your plate, and treating your time as capital means routing the low-return work elsewhere whenever you can.
3. Speak to your boss about reviewing your workload. If your current load genuinely exceeds what any reasonable capacity can sustain, that's a conversation to have explicitly, not something to absorb silently.

**Strategic question:** If you kept operating at this load for another year, which would break first: your health, your relationships, or your judgment?

---

### diag_sp4_B1
**Badge:** MISALLOCATED ENERGY  
**Title:** The Real Problem Could Be What You're Doing, Not How Much You're Doing

**Your situation:** Your energy is going into things you didn't choose, like politics, busywork or low-value meetings, and a meaningful share of it gets absorbed before it ever reaches the work that actually matters.

**Why this is a challenge:** Energy management is capital allocation. When a meaningful share of your capacity is being routed into low-return activities by other people's decisions, personal discipline on your end won't fix it, because the drain is structural, not behavioural.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Map where your energy actually goes for one week and flag what's imposed versus genuinely necessary. You can't push back on a drain you haven't named.
2. Take the imposed low-value items to whoever assigns them and ask directly what can be delegated, cut, or restructured. Most of these continue simply because nobody has questioned them out loud.
3. Protect your highest-return hours for your highest-return work, and push the unavoidable low-value drains into whatever time is left over, not the other way round.

**Strategic question:** Whose calendar are you actually working from this week, yours or everyone else's?

---

### diag_sp4_B2
**Badge:** SELF-IMPOSED DRAIN  
**Title:** You Might Be Wearing Exhaustion As A Badge Of Honour

**Your situation:** Much of your energy is going toward things you took on yourself, like saying yes to everything or feeling you need to prove you're working hard, rather than things anyone actually required of you.

**Why this is a challenge:** This is one of the most common mistakes among senior professionals. Wearing exhaustion as a badge of honour feels like commitment, but it's Energy Currency being spent with no return, and ignoring this can trigger cascading failure across every other currency you hold.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Separate what's required from what you've voluntarily added on top. Most self-imposed load lives in that second category.
2. Practice saying no to one thing this week that nobody actually asked you to take on, and notice how little actually breaks as a result.
3. Redefine what "working hard" looks like for you. High-return activity, not visible exhaustion, is the real signal of strong Energy Currency management.

**Strategic question:** If nobody was watching, how much of what you're currently doing would you still choose to do?

## Situation 5: I'm having trouble with someone at work

### diag_sp5_A1
**Badge:** TRUST BREAKDOWN  
**Title:** Your Boss Might Be Breaking The Trust Your Reputation Runs On

**Your situation:** Someone above you, a manager or senior leader, is actively undermining you: broken promises, emotional outbursts, or inconsistent backing that's eroding the trust your reputation depends on.

**Why this is a challenge:** Reputation Currency is the trust and confidence others have in your ability, judgement and character, and it converts your skills and experience into sponsorship and opportunity. It can take years to build and one serious breach to weaken, and when the breach comes from someone with power over you, the damage compounds faster than you can repair it alone.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Document what's happening. Broken promises and inconsistent backing are hard to act on from memory alone, and a clear record protects you if this needs to go further.
2. Find allies. Look for other sponsors or senior leaders you can build a relationship with, so your standing doesn't rest entirely on this one person's goodwill.
3. Decide how much longer you're willing to let one relationship control your Reputation Currency. If the answer is "not much," start building your next move in parallel.

**Strategic question:** If this relationship doesn't change, how much of your Energy Currency are you willing to let it cost you?

---

### diag_sp5_A2
**Badge:** SPONSORSHIP GAP  
**Title:** You Might Be Doing Good Work That Nobody In Power Is Backing

**Your situation:** Your manager or senior leadership isn't working against you, but they're also not investing in you: no sponsorship, no visibility, no real backing when it matters.

**Why this is a challenge:** Reputation Currency converts your skills and experience into sponsorship and opportunity, but it doesn't convert itself. Without someone above you willing to back you, your work stays invisible at the level where promotions and high-visibility opportunities actually get decided.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Get explicit about what sponsorship would actually look like, and ask for it directly. Many managers won't volunteer it until you name what you need.
2. Build visible outcomes deliberately, not just reliable delivery. Reputation runs on consistency and clear communication, but it also needs to be seen to count.
3. If this manager genuinely isn't going to invest in you, find another internal sponsor or build your external reputation so you're not dependent on one person's goodwill.

**Strategic question:** If your current manager left tomorrow, would anyone above you have noticed your work enough to advocate for you?

---

### diag_sp5_B1
**Badge:** NETWORK GAP  
**Title:** You Might Be Missing The Trust And Access Your Peers Already Have

**Your situation:** You're not being attacked by your colleagues, but you're also not part of the inner circle: the visibility, trust, and goodwill that flow between peers seem to flow around you instead.

**Why this is a challenge:** Network Currency runs on visibility, strategic alignment, trust, and positive investment, the goodwill that makes people inclined to help you. Without it, you lose access to the hidden job market, early information, and the kind of lateral support that makes a professional harder to sideline.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Stop approaching this as "who can help me" and start with "how can I help." The best networks are built on usefulness and goodwill, not a transaction.
2. Start with warm, low-stakes touchpoints rather than trying to force your way into an existing group. Small, consistent contact builds trust faster than one big gesture.
3. Get deliberate about visibility. People can't trust or include you if they don't know what you stand for and where you're heading.

**Strategic question:** Whose goodwill have you actually invested in this year, and whose have you just assumed?

---

### diag_sp5_B2
**Badge:** PEER SABOTAGE  
**Title:** A Peer Might Be Acting Against You

**Your situation:** A colleague at your level is actively working against you: taking credit, undermining you in front of others, or otherwise spending your reputation to build theirs.

**Why this is a challenge:** Reputation is fragile. It can take years to build and one serious breach to weaken, and lateral sabotage is just as damaging as a breach from above, because peers often have just as much visibility into your work and just as much influence on how it's perceived.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Make your contributions visible through your own channels: direct updates to stakeholders, written records, named credit, rather than relying on this peer to represent you accurately.
2. Address it directly with the person first, plainly and without escalation, since many conflicts get worse from assumed intent rather than confirmed sabotage.
3. If it continues after a direct conversation, loop in whoever has visibility on both of you, and let your documented track record do the talking.

**Strategic question:** If this person keeps taking credit for your work, who will actually notice unless you make it visible yourself?

## Situation 6: I'm worried my skills aren't good enough

### diag_sp6_A1
**Badge:** CLARITY GAP  
**Title:** You Might Not Actually Know What You're Good At

**Your situation:** You're not sure what skills you genuinely have, or how strong they really are, and that uncertainty is making it hard to know what to fix or where to play.

**Why this is a challenge:** Skill is the ability to apply knowledge to perform a task, and competency is doing that reliably under real consequences. Without an honest read on which of these you actually have, you can't tell the difference between a skill you're confident about and one you've simply never been tested on.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Separate what you know from what you can actually do. Attending a course gives you knowledge, not skill, and the two get confused more often than people admit.
2. Get honest feedback from people who've seen you work under real stakes, not just self-assessment. Competency only shows up when there are real consequences attached.
3. Audit your skills against what makes Skills Currency strong: is it in demand, current, demonstrable, and useful in the market you actually want to play in.

**Strategic question:** If someone asked you to prove your strongest skill tomorrow, under real consequences, could you?

---

### diag_sp6_A2
**Badge:** MARKET BLIND SPOT  
**Title:** You Might Know What You Can Do, Just Not Where It Is Truly Valued

**Your situation:** You know your skills reasonably well, but you're not sure whether they're actually in demand, or what the market is willing to pay for them right now.

**Why this is a challenge:** Skill relevance is contextual. The same capability can be highly valuable in one industry and ordinary in another, and a skill that isn't connected to a real market need stays a capability sitting on a shelf, not a currency you can actually spend.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Ask three direct questions about every skill you're relying on: who needs this, what problem does it solve, and what is the market actually willing to pay for it.
2. Look at cross-skilling and skill stacking. Combinations like operations plus sustainability, or finance plus commercial storytelling, are often more differentiated and in demand than any single skill on its own.
3. Talk to people actually hiring in your target market, not just people in your current one, since relevance shifts the moment you change industries or functions.

**Strategic question:** If you had to sell your strongest skill to a market you've never worked in, would anyone actually buy it?

---

### diag_sp6_B
**Badge:** SKILL DECAY  
**Title:** A Skill You're Relying On Might Already Be Getting Outdated

**Your situation:** A skill you've leaned on for years, whether due to natural half-life, your own complacency, or a shift like AI or industry change, might no longer be as valuable as you assume.

**Why this is a challenge:** Technical and functional skills have shorter half-lives than most people expect, and one of the most common mistakes professionals make is assuming past skills will remain valuable indefinitely. A skill doesn't announce its own expiry. It simply stops getting you what it used to, often well before you notice.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Audit what's gone stale. Pick your most-relied-on skill and check honestly whether it's still current, in demand, and demonstrable, or whether you've been coasting on its old reputation.
2. Keep updating deliberately. Whether the cause is natural decay, complacency, or an external force, the fix is the same: treat updating your skills as ongoing, not a one-time investment.
3. Don't wait for an obvious trigger like a layoff or a tech shift to force the update. By the time it's obvious, you're already behind.

**Strategic question:** Which of your current skills are you still trading on simply because they used to work?

---

### diag_sp6_C1
**Badge:** TRANSLATION GAP  
**Title:** You Might Have The Skill, Just Not The Proof

**Your situation:** You believe you have this skill, or even hold a certificate for it, but you've never actually been tested on it under real pressure or real consequences.

**Why this is a challenge:** A person can claim skill without proving competence under real consequences, and collecting certificates as proof of skill is one of the most common mistakes professionals make. Until a skill has been applied on something with real stakes, it's still closer to knowledge than competency, no matter how confident you feel about it.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Volunteer for a stretch assignment or project where this specific skill will actually be tested, with real consequences attached, not just practiced in a low-stakes setting.
2. Treat trial and error in real work as part of the build, not a risk to avoid. Competency is built through application, not certification.
3. Be honest with yourself about the difference between having learned something and having delivered with it, and stop presenting the first as if it were the second.

**Strategic question:** If you had to apply this skill tomorrow with real consequences riding on it, would you actually trust yourself?

---

### diag_sp6_C2
**Badge:** ARTICULATION GAP  
**Title:** You Might Have The Proof, Just Not The Story

**Your situation:** You've genuinely proven this skill through real outcomes, but you struggle to articulate it or show it to the people who'd actually need to know about it.

**Why this is a challenge:** Having demonstrable outcomes is one of the core qualities that makes Skills Currency strong, and failing to convert skills into experience that others can see is one of the most common mistakes professionals make. A skill nobody can see or verify functions the same as a skill that doesn't exist, no matter how real the outcome was.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Convert your projects into narratives of impact: what the situation was, what you did, and what measurable outcome resulted. A result without a story attached doesn't travel.
2. Practice explaining this skill out loud, to a recruiter, a peer, or a manager, until the explanation is as sharp as the work itself.
3. Document achievements as they happen using measurable outcomes, rather than trying to reconstruct the story months or years later when the details have faded.

**Strategic question:** If you couldn't show your resume or your title, could you still convince someone you're good at this?
