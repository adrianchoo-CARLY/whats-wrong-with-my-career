# Result cards: situation 7, trouble landing a job (22 cards)

These are the result cards. Present the matched card faithfully: keep its title, its situation, its why, its three actions and its strategic question. You may lightly adapt wording to the person's own words, but do not add statistics, new frameworks or promises that are not in the card. Card ids are internal; never show them to the person.


### js_diag_network
**Badge:** NETWORK GAP  
**Title:** Your Network Currency Is Dangerously Low

**Your situation:** You're reaching out, even to people who already know you, and the replies just don't come back. Honestly, silence from someone you thought was a friend stings even more than a rejection from a stranger.

**Why this is a challenge:** You need to realise that a contact list is not a network. Many roles are filled through referrals and real conversations before they're ever advertised, and a weak network leaves you competing with strangers for whatever is left.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Start with people who already trust you, ex colleagues, alumni, old clients... not strangers on LinkedIn.
2. Lead every message with a tight, current story of what you've been doing and what you're after - don't ask for a role - remember, you are not a job beggar!
3. Flip the question in your head from “who can help me” to “how can I help you first.” People feel that difference, even over a short message.

**Strategic question:** Of the last ten people you reached out to, how many had actually heard from you in the two years before you needed something?

### js_diag_story_cv
**Badge:** CV STORY  
**Title:** Perhaps Your CV Isn't Telling The Right Career Story

**Your situation:** Your CV goes out, but nothing comes back and you start wondering if you've become unemployable. Don't worry, it's usually not the case... most likely, your CV just isn't telling anyone the right story about you.

**Why this is a challenge:** A recruiter often gives your CV only a quick first skim, and in that window, if your resume isn't telling the right story about you, they're moving on to the next candidate.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Rebuild it around your Commercial Contribution: how you've created value over the years, the specific achievements you delivered, and make them count!
2. Be brief and concise! Cut anything that doesn't build toward the role you're applying for, even if it was a real and meaningful chapter of your career.
3. Customise it for every single application. Recruiters can usually tell when you're using a boiler-room template, or an unedited AI-generated one.

**Strategic question:** If parts of your CV are more than 3 years old, isn't it time you did a total reboot from scratch?

### js_diag_ats
**Badge:** ATS BLOCK  
**Title:** Your Application May Be Filtered Out By The ATS/AI System

**Your situation:** You've written a strong CV, and you're still hearing nothing. Before you start doubting yourself, check whether a human ever actually saw it.

**Why this is a challenge:** Many companies now run every CV through an AI Applicant Tracking System before a person looks at it. Get the format wrong, and a perfectly good candidate just disappears into the system.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Mirror the exact keywords and job titles in the job ad, not the synonyms you'd personally choose.
2. Strip out the fancy graphics, columns and text boxes. They look sharp to a human eye and unreadable to the AI software.
3. Send it as a sensibly named, sensibly sized Word or PDF file, with standard headers like “Experience” and “Education,” not creative ones.

**Strategic question:** If a machine read your CV before any human did, would it find the exact words this job ad is screening for?

### js_diag_narrow_nicher
**Badge:** SHRINKING NICHE  
**Title:** You're A Narrow Nicher - Has Your Market Moved On Without You?

**Your situation:** You may have built real depth in something specific, but now that specific something might have disappeared from the market , and that's why the opportunities are fewer now.

**Why this is a challenge:** A niche that pays well today can vanish in a few years, and when it does, the very specialisation that once made you valuable becomes the thing limiting your options.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Run an honest Skills Currency audit. Is what you do still deep, still wanted, still rare, or has it become commoditised, or even replaceable by AI?
2. Cross-skill deliberately into something adjacent, so your whole career isn't riding on one shrinking niche again.
3. Look at a Quadrant 3 move, same industry, different role, so you keep your domain knowledge while changing what you're actually selling.

**Strategic question:** What are your true skills and experience... and can you find another market for them? What are your adjacencies?

### js_diag_expensive_exec
**Badge:** PRICED OUT  
**Title:** You Might Be A 'Too Experienced' Executive

**Your situation:** You did everything right and climbed for years, and now you're hearing “you're a bit senior for this” more often than you'd like. That sentence stings every time, no matter how many times it comes.

**Why this is a challenge:** The market's open roles have clustered below where your compensation sits, and you need a better strategy to address this gap.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Get a real, external valuation. Call two or three headhunters in your sector and ask plainly what they could place you at today.
2. Decide deliberately whether this is the moment for a Downshift, trading some pay and intensity for sustainability, framed as a strategic choice, not a retreat.
3. Consider unbundling into a portfolio: advisory, board work or project consulting can sometimes beat chasing one role at your former compensation level.

**Strategic question:** Are you being too selective about roles, or are your salary expectations out of step with today's market?

### js_diag_muddled_middleman
**Badge:** UNCLEAR VALUE  
**Title:** You're Having An Identity Crisis - You Can't Name What You Really Are

**Your situation:** You spent years coordinating, managing, holding things together, and now you're struggling to name exactly what you actually own. You've become a Swiss Army Knife, but the market wants a very specialised surgical tool.

**Why this is a challenge:** Roles built purely around coordinating other people's work are the first to be automated or absorbed, because the value being exchanged was never clearly yours to begin with. You need to identify your Skills and Experience Currencies.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Name the one deep skill sitting underneath all that coordinating, budgeting, vendor negotiation, process design, whatever it is, and build your story around that, not the job title.
2. Deep-skill into it on purpose, until you're the person people come to for that specific thing, not just the one who runs the meetings.
3. In interviews, be specific with your achievements... and highlight your commercial outcomes.

**Strategic question:** If your role disappeared tomorrow, what's the one capability a company would still need to buy from someone?

### js_diag_disrupted_dinosaur
**Badge:** FUNCTION DISRUPTED  
**Title:** You Might Be A Disrupted Dinosaur

**Your situation:** You did your job well for many years, and now the entire function is being automated or shipped offshore. This one's hard, and honestly, it isn't about effort or performance at all.

**Why this is a challenge:** When an entire function moves, the years of loyalty and competence behind it don't disappear, they just stop being what the market is currently buying.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Stop competing with the automation directly. Look at the judgement-heavy, exception-handling, relationship work that still needs a human.
2. Treat this as a genuine Pivot (Quadrant 3) or Reboot (Quadrant 4), with realistic expectations on pace and pay, not a quick lateral patch.
3. Lean on your Reputation and Network Currency to get a foot in the door even at a lower level. Deep domain credibility often beats a missing line on a resume.

**Strategic question:** What's one piece of judgement or relationship work in your old function that no algorithm has touched yet?

### js_diag_career_story
**Badge:** UNCLEAR STORY  
**Title:** You Didn't Answer “Who Is This Person?” Clearly Enough

**Your situation:** You walk out of interviews feeling confident, then hear nothing, or worse, “we just didn't get a clear read on you.” That's a particularly frustrating kind of rejection.

**Why this is a challenge:** Every interview is really three hidden questions, and the first - who is this person - only gets answered if you've built your Career Story on purpose. Without it, the interviewer is left assembling your history themselves, and badly.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Write a sixty to ninety second version of your career that ends at this exact job, not a chronological tour through every employer you've ever had.
2. Reframe each past role as something that was, in hindsight, building toward what this company needs right now.
3. Cut anything that doesn't serve your Positioning, even if it was a real and proud chapter of your career.

**Strategic question:** If you had exactly sixty seconds to make a stranger understand your career, what would you actually say?

### js_diag_success_stories
**Badge:** PROOF GAP  
**Title:** You Didn't Quite Assure Them You Can Do The Job

**Your situation:** They liked you in the room, and the offer still didn't come. Somewhere between “I like this person” and “I trust this person to deliver,” the interviewer lost confidence.

**Why this is a challenge:** Interviewers buy a track record, not a job description. A vague answer like "I led the project" raises more doubt about what you actually contributed.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Build at least five success stories using our Six-step Model Answer: the achievement, the root cause you found, the solution and why, the obstacle (usually people), the specific outcome, and the lesson.
2. Use real numbers wherever you can. “Reduced costs” is forgettable; a specific figure and timeframe is memorable.
3. Never embellish. A sharp interviewer will be able to detect deception very quickly.

**Strategic question:** If an interviewer pushed back hard on your best story right now, how would you respond?

### js_diag_value_prop
**Badge:** VALUE PITCH  
**Title:** You Couldn't Answer “What Can You Do For Me?” Well Enough

**Your situation:** “Great candidate, just not for this role” is one of the more confusing things to hear, because it sounds like a compliment and a rejection at the same time.

**Why this is a challenge:** An interview is a sales pitch, and buyers only care about what benefits them specifically. A generic pitch, however polished, doesn't answer their actual problem.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Before you walk in, find out the company's growth strategy, its three biggest current headaches, and why this role exists in the first place.
2. Tailor your Career Story and success stories to those exact needs, not the generic version you used last week for a different company.
3. Think in terms of the 4X Principle. Be ready to show, concretely, how you'd create several multiples of the value of your salary in this specific seat.

**Strategic question:** Could you name, right now, the single biggest problem this employer needs solved in the next twelve months?

### js_diag_culture_fit
**Badge:** FIT MISMATCH  
**Title:** This Role Might Not Be The Best Fit For You

**Your situation:** You gave strong answers, but the fit just wasn't there anyway. Honestly, this one isn't a failure on your side.

**Why this is a challenge:** Sometimes the mismatch is real - working style, pace, values - and no amount of polish closes that particular gap. Pretending otherwise just sets you up for failure at the new company.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Get honest with yourself about your own Values and Personality fit. What pace, dynamic and decision-making style do you actually thrive in?
2. Use the interview itself to test fit on purpose: ask about team dynamics, how conflict gets handled, how decisions actually get made.
3. If you keep getting rejected for the same type of roles, treat that as a targeting signal. It’s time to re-examine your job-search strategy.

**Strategic question:** Is there a pattern in the types of companies where you keep hitting this same wall?

### js_diag_due_diligence
**Badge:** OFFER DUE DILIGENCE  
**Title:** You Could Be Ignoring The Warning Signs

**Your situation:** You find the red flags only after you've signed, and by then it's expensive to undo. Your lack of research is the actual problem here, not your judgement.

**Why this is a challenge:** Almost every red flag that surfaces after accepting was findable before accepting, if the right questions had been asked at the right moment.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Do your due diligence properly before signing anything: the company's financial stability, culture, repeated reorganisations, and the actual team you'll work with, not just the hiring manager.
2. Get every vague clause (e.g. stock options, bonus structure, non-competition) sorted in writing before you sign, not after.
3. Speak to people with personal or past insights into this new company to get the unfiltered version of what it’s like to work there.

**Strategic question:** What's the one aspect about the new role you are most unsure of?

### js_diag_salary_negotiation
**Badge:** NEGOTIATION GAP  
**Title:** Your Salary Negotiation Skills Need Improving

**Your situation:** The money conversation arrives, and something in you freezes or fumbles it. It's never easy, but there's actually a way to negotiate.

**Why this is a challenge:** Without a script, the conversation defaults to whoever speaks first, and that's rarely you. A clear plan changes the entire dynamic in the room.

**Three things you can do:**

1. When asked your expected salary, ask for their range first, or come in with a number you'd genuinely be happy with. Don't guess defensively.
2. Know the market benchmark before you walk in. Check salary surveys and talk to recruiters in your sector, rather than anchoring only on your current pay, especially if you're already underpriced.
3. Negotiate base, bonus, sign-on and equity as separate conversations, not one number lumped together.

**Strategic question:** What's your actual walk-away number, decided before the call, not during it?

### js_diag_counteroffer
**Badge:** COUNTER-OFFER TRAP  
**Title:** You're Caught In The Counter-offer Trap

**Your situation:** You resign, the counteroffer lands on the table, and suddenly you're stuck deciding all over again. It's an uncomfortable, very human spot to be in.

**Why this is a challenge:** Many people who accept a counteroffer end up leaving anyway, sometimes having strained relationships with both employers in the process.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Ask yourself plainly why you weren't worth that money yesterday. If the honest answer is “because I threatened to leave,” that dynamic doesn't go away.
2. Remember it can be a delay tactic that buys your employer time to find your replacement, while you've now flagged yourself as a flight risk.
3. Resign to notify, not to negotiate. If you're genuinely torn after resigning, that's a sign the original decision wasn't fully resolved before you handed in your notice.

**Strategic question:** If the counteroffer hadn't appeared at all, would you still want to stay?

### js_diag_q2
**Badge:** INDUSTRY PIVOT  
**Title:** You're Planning An Industry Pivot

**Your situation:** You're aiming for the same role in a new industry, and the worry of “will they even take me seriously” is sitting heavy. That's a reasonable worry, and a manageable one.

**Why this is a challenge:** Your functional skills are genuinely transferable here. The real risk is underestimating how different the new industry's politics, buyers and unwritten rules actually are.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Build strong Agility and Network Currency in the target industry. You need someone on the inside willing to vouch for the translation.
2. Rehearse a clear story for why you can do the job, articulating your underlying skills that are directly transferable.
3. Research the new industry's specific pressures before every interview, so you don't appear to be uninformed.

**Strategic question:** Who do you actually know inside your target industry who could vouch for you today?

### js_diag_q3
**Badge:** ROLE PIVOT  
**Title:** You're Trying A Role Pivot

**Your situation:** You're staying in the same industry but reaching for a different role. You need to prove you can actually do it, not just understand it. That gap is there, and you need to figure out how to bridge it.

**Why this is a challenge:** Employers need proof you can do the new function, and domain knowledge alone doesn't supply that proof. This move typically takes longer than people expect.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Lean on strong Reputation Currency in the industry, ideally a sponsor willing to take a chance on you for the new function.
2. Surface concrete examples of related tasks you've already done, rather than assuming your domain expertise speaks for itself.
3. Stay open to a smaller first step into the new role, rather than expecting to land the full target position in one move.

**Strategic question:** What's one piece of evidence you already have that proves you can do the job?

### js_diag_q4
**Badge:** CAREER REBOOT  
**Title:** You're Doing A Complete Career Switch - Be Careful

**Your situation:** You're trying to change both your role and your industry at once… and frankly, this is the hardest move on the board. If it feels heavy, that's because it genuinely is.

**Why this is a challenge:** A full reboot means relearning the role and the industry at the same time, and often, the learning curve can be quite unforgiving.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Build unusually strong Reputation, Network, Financial and Energy Currency before you start. A pivot like this takes time and effort to incubate, so plan carefully.
2. Get honest about your motive. Is this a real pull toward something new, or a push to escape your current situation? Reboots driven by escapism tend to fail.
3. Accept a realistic entry point, even apprentice-level pay or a lower title, and treat it as a deliberate career move, not a step backward.

**Strategic question:** If your Financial Currency ran out in six months, would you still choose this move?

### js_diag_currency_audit
**Badge:** CURRENCY AUDIT  
**Title:** Run A Career Currency Audit Before Doing Anything Else

**Your situation:** You're not actively job hunting, and still that nagging feeling of being replaceable won't go away. Frankly, that instinct is usually worth listening to.

**Why this is a challenge:** Time in a role doesn't automatically build value. Repetition without progression is stagnation... it could even make you more complacent!

**Three things you can do:**

1. Audit across all seven Career Currencies - Skills, Experience, Reputation, Network, Agility, Energy, Financial - with outside input, not just self-assessment.
2. Ask the harder question separately: what would the market actually pay for what you have right now? Test it with a real headhunter conversation, not a guess.
3. Identify the one currency most likely to be your weak link, and grow it deliberately this quarter.

**Strategic question:** If you lost your job tomorrow, which currency would you discover was weaker than you thought?

### js_diag_lion
**Badge:** CAREER LION  
**Title:** Roar - You're A Career Lion!

**Your situation:** You've got a real plan and the ability to execute on it. Honestly, you're in a good position, and the real danger now, ironically, is getting too comfortable inside it.

**Why this is a challenge:** Strong strategy and strong agility together usually mean you're not actually stuck - you're tactically off somewhere specific, which is a far easier fix.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Keep your edge by broadening into adjacent industries or skillsets before you need to, not after the market forces your hand.
2. Network deliberately with other strong performers for early trend spotting. Lions who isolate lose their lead first.
3. If offers still aren't landing despite this, look at the tactical layer - your CV, your Career Story, your interview pitch - because the strategy itself likely isn't the problem.

**Strategic question:** When was the last time you genuinely tested your market value, rather than assumed it?

### js_diag_trex
**Badge:** CAREER T-REX  
**Title:** You're A Career T-Rex: Strong Strategy, But Not Agile Enough

**Your situation:** You know exactly what you want, but you keep hitting the same wall trying to get there. It's frustrating, and that kind of repeated near miss is getting exhausting.

**Why this is a challenge:** A strong career plan paired with weak agility usually means the gap isn't in your thinking…it’s in execution, skills, network or positioning that haven't kept pace with the plan.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Run a Currency Audit specifically on Agility. Are your skills genuinely current and in demand and is your network warm enough to open doors?
2. Check your positioning. Are you seen as someone who's outgrown the old role, or defined by it? That perception is usually the real hurdle, not the strategy.
3. Consider whether the plan itself needs revising…are you being realistic?

**Strategic question:** Is the wall you keep hitting about your skills, your network, or how you're perceived?

### js_diag_canary
**Badge:** CAREER CANARY  
**Title:** You're A Career Canary: Capable, But Too Complacent

**Your situation:** You're capable, you just haven't needed a plan, because the company has been managing your career for you. That's comfortable… until the day it suddenly isn't.

**Why this is a challenge:** All is good until your sponsor retires or a reorganisation hits... then Career Canaries can go from secure to exposed almost overnight.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Ask yourself plainly: what would happen if I were retrenched tomorrow? If that answer is unsettling, that's the signal to build a Career Strategy now, while you still have leverage.
2. Prepare an exit plan before you need one. A network gone cold takes months to rebuild, and you don't want to start from zero in a crisis.
3. Get an external valuation of your skills now, while you're still employed and not negotiating from a position of urgency.

**Strategic question:** If your current sponsor or company disappeared tomorrow, who else actually knows what you're capable of?

### js_diag_panda
**Badge:** CAREER PANDA  
**Title:** You're A Career Panda: No Strategy, Limited Agility

**Your situation:** You're in a precarious situation where there's no real plan beyond showing up and staying put. You're stagnating in a dangerous way.

**Why this is a challenge:** This profile is often among the most exposed in a reorganisation, and the discomfort of admitting that is exactly what keeps people frozen in it longer than they should be.

**Three things you can do:**

1. Run a Skills Audit. How many of your skills are current, in demand, transferable, not easily replicated by someone cheaper or by automation? Be brutally honest with yourself.
2. Pick the single biggest gap and start closing it this week, not next quarter. Small action now beats a perfect plan later.
3. Start with a very basic Career Strategy. Even a simple plan will evolve into something more concrete later.

**Strategic question:** What's stopped you from starting on this until now?
